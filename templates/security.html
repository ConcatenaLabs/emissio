Security rewards

Break it on the testnet, not at launch

Report vulnerabilities in the Sequentia node, the wallets, SeqDEX, SeqLN, or the project infrastructure. Reports are private: only you and the review team can see them. Accepted reports are credited in SEQ according to severity, and a proposed fix raises the award within its tier. {{seq .Data.Reserve}} SEQ of the program pool is reserved for security awards.

Amounts are the reference award for a clear, reproducible report in that tier. The review team sets the final award. The first reporter of an issue is the one rewarded; later reports of the same issue are marked duplicates.

What every accepted report needs

  • A defect in shipped code. The node, the wallets, SeqDEX, SeqLN or the project's public services, as released or on the default branch. Documentation, advice and configuration examples are not in scope here.
  • A security consequence you demonstrate. Show what an attacker gains or a user loses, even if it is small. "Could be dangerous combined with an unknown bug" does not qualify, and neither do missing headers, version disclosure, rate limits, or best-practice deviations without an exploit.
  • Reproduction a reviewer can confirm in under an hour from the steps as written. A report that cannot be reproduced is not accepted at any tier.
  • First and untracked. Anything already on the public issue trackers or fixed on the default branch is a duplicate.

Found a real bug without a security angle? The Report a bug task pays for those.

Exceptional findings

A finding can be awarded far above the Critical tier, up to {{seq .Data.Max}} SEQ, when all of the following hold: the consequences are network-wide (a consensus split, silent inflation of an asset, theft of funds without user interaction, or a break of Bitcoin anchoring), the technique is truly novel rather than a known class applied to new code, and the report comes with a working proof of concept. The team decides these awards case by case, and the reasoning is shared with the reporter.

Ground rules

  • Test against your own nodes and wallets, or the public testnet. Never against other users or their funds.
  • No denial of service against shared infrastructure beyond the minimum needed to prove the issue.
  • Report privately here first. Public disclosure before a fix ships forfeits the reward.
  • Include steps to reproduce. A working proof of concept and a proposed fix both raise the award.
